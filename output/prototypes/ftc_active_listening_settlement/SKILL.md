# FTC "Active Listening" Settlement Analysis

Provides analysis and context on the FTC's May 2026 enforcement action against Cox Media Group (CMG), MindSift, and 1010 Digital Works for deceiving customers about an "Active Listening" AI-powered marketing service.

- "What happened with the FTC active listening case?"

## How to use

### 1. Understand the core deception

CMG, MindSift, and 1010 Digital Works marketed an "Active Listening" service claiming:
- Smart devices capture real-time intent data by listening to consumers' conversations
- Advertisers could pair voice data with behavioral data to target in-market consumers

**In reality**, the service did **not** listen to conversations or use voice data at all. It relied on standard ad-targeting techniques (behavioral data, cookies, demographic profiling) rebranded with a misleading "active listening" label.

### 2. Key findings

- The companies deceived both advertisers (their customers) and alarmed consumers
- The pitch deck language about "voice data" was marketing hype, not technical reality
- The service also failed to accurately place ads in customers' desired locations
- The FTC settlement required nearly $1 million in penalties across the three firms

### 3. Regulatory and industry implications

- **For marketers**: Do not overstate AI capabilities in sales materials. Claiming technology does something it cannot constitutes deceptive trade practices under FTC Act Section 5.
- **For consumers**: While the "phones are listening" fear is widespread, this case confirms the targeting comes from behavioral data, not microphone access.
- **For AI companies**: FTC is actively enforcing against misleading AI capability claims, part of a broader pattern of AI-related enforcement actions.

### 4. Timeline

- **2024**: CMG pitch deck surfaces publicly, claiming "active listening" via smart devices
- **September 2024**: Security researchers and journalists (including Simon Willison) analyze the claims and hypothesize the tech was standard ad targeting rebranded
- **May 2026**: FTC announces settlement confirming the service never used voice data

### 5. Apply lessons to your work

When reviewing AI marketing claims or building AI product messaging:
- Ensure technical claims match actual system capabilities
- Avoid aspirational language that implies functionality not yet built
- Review pitch decks and sales materials for regulatory compliance
- Document what your AI system actually does vs. what marketing says it does
